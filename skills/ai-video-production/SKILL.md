# AI video production

## Trigger
Use when planning or generating videos with Google Flow/Veo, Runway, Kling, or similar tools, including multi-clip stories and product explainers.

## Workflow
1. Establish the objective, platform, audience, aspect ratio, duration, language, and CTA.
2. Separate verified product facts from creative fiction. Never invent app screens, features, prices, user counts, or outcomes.
3. Write the complete script first. Keep dialogue exact; specify pronunciation, speaker, emotion, pace, and lip-sync requirements.
4. Build a continuity bible: character appearance, clothing, voice, location, props, lighting, camera geography, color grade, and time of day.
5. Break the story into tool-compatible clips. For this creator's default workflow, use 10-second clips unless the platform or user requests another duration.
6. For every clip specify: opening frame, action by time, camera movement, dialogue, expression, graphics, sound effects, music, ending frame, and transition into the next clip.
7. Repeat critical continuity details in every prompt. Do not assume a video model remembers earlier clips.
8. Add negative constraints: no extra dialogue, no gibberish text, no warped faces/hands, no unwanted objects, no sudden wardrobe/scene changes.
9. Plan captions and on-screen text separately; verify spelling and rendering after generation.
10. Deliver an edit-ready sequence with clip order, transitions, audio cues, captions, and final CTA.

## Quality gate
- Character, voice, wardrobe, and scene remain consistent.
- Dialogue is verbatim and plausible to lip-sync.
- Every visual claim is accurate.
- Motion and camera direction are physically coherent.
- Music and SFX support the emotion without overpowering speech.
- Output is framed for the target platform.

## Cost-aware practice
Prototype one short clip before generating a full sequence. Prefer free tiers and test credits, but disclose watermarks, quotas, and limitations. Never promise unlimited or free generation without checking current terms.
